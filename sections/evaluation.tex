To assess the feasibility of the \ourmethodshort{}, in this study a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed solution have been applied. 

\subsection{Models}
For the evaluation purposes six \llms{} with different number of parameters were chosen to estimate the performance on different scales \llms{}: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \gpt{} with 24 Transformer blocks;
    \item \llamaTwoSmall{} with 32 Transformer blocks;
    \item \llamaTwoBig{} with 40 Transformer blocks;
    \item \qwenSmall{} with 36 Transformer blocks;
    \item \qwenMedium{} with 28 Transformer blocks;
    \item \qwenLarge{} with 64 Transformer blocks.
\end{itemize}
The \gpt{} model was taken from \textit{transformers v4.33.2},  \llamaTwoSmall{}, \llamaTwoBig{}, \qwenSmall{}, \qwenMedium{}, \qwenLarge{} models were taken from HuggingFace hub \cite{huggingface:llama2-7b}, \cite{huggingface:llama2-13b}, \cite{huggingface:qwen-small}, \cite{huggingface:qwen-medium}, \cite{huggingface:qwen-large}.

\subsection{Overview of considered compretitors}
To validate the efficacy of \ourmethodshort{}, a comparative analysis was employed with alternative approaches in the domain of 
fine-tuning acceleration and importance analysis of \llm{} layers and Transformer blocks.

Examined methods to analyze the importance of Transformer blocks include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \uidl{} \cite{uidl} approach. After estimating the most important model layers during the inference on calibration data and further pruning of the least essential struc-tures, \uidl{} utilizes QLoRA fine-tuning to enhance model quality. 
    \item The \sleb{} \cite{sleb} approach.
    \item The \shortllama{} \cite{kim2024shortenedllamadepthpruning} approach. 
    \item The \ows{} \cite{ows} approach. After estimating the importance during the inference on calibration data and further pruning of the least essential structures, \ows{} utilizes full fine-tuning to enhance model quality.
    \item The \lisa{} \cite{lisa} approach, which randomly selects Transformer blocks during the process of full fine-tuning. This method does not rely on any deliberate heuristic to estimate the importance, that is why it can be considered as the reference approach for understanding the effect of the "importance". 
\end{enumerate}
Notable, all these approaches, except \lisa{}, employ different heuristics to estimate Transformer blocks importance, which could provide insights into the effectiveness of existing metrics and their correlation with \llm{} performance. Also, it helps to evaluate whether using these importance measures can optimize the fine-tuning process compared to \lora{}. For practical implementation, the official implementation of considered approaches was taken from corresponding Github repositories: \uidl{} \cite{uidl_github}, \sleb{} \cite{sleb_github}, \shortllama{} \cite{short_llama_github}, \ows{} \cite{ows}, \lisa{} \cite{lisa}.

Since \lora{} is the cutting-edge approach for fine-tuning acceleration, it was selec-ted as a baseline for computational experiments on quality and acceleration of conside-red approaches. In addition, the most prominent modifications of \lora{}, such as \dora{} \cite{dora}, \pissa{} \cite{pissa}, and \adalora{} \cite{adalora}, also \lisa{} \cite{lisa} were chosen as reference methods during the evaluation. The implementation of \lora{}, \dora{}, \pissa{}, and \adalora{} approaches was taken from \textit{peft v.0.18.0} library \cite{peft}.

\subsection{Training and evaluation protocols}
Firstly, for the \uidl{}, \sleb{}, \shortllama{}, and \ows{} approaches the impor-tance estimation of Transformer blocks was conducted on a validation set of \alpaca{} dataset. Subsequently, for the \uidl{} the QLoRA fine-tuning was applied to key Transformer blocks, while non-essential ones were left unchanged.
In contrast, for \ows{} the full fine-tuning of critical units was utilized, while others were frozen. Originally, \sleb{} and \shortllama{} do not employ fine-tuning to improve model performance. However, following the pruning of non-essential Transformer blocks, which involved their total removal, the \lora{} fine-tuning method was utilized to important Transformer blocks.

Computational experiments with chosen models and approaches are presentend in Table \ref{tab:quality_1}, Table \ref{tab:quality_2}, and Table \ref{tab:acceleration}. The best results of fine-tuning acceleration are presented in Table \ref{tab:acceleration}, while the best results on quality are presented in Table \ref{tab:quality_1} and Table \ref{tab:quality_2}, where for \textit{Wikitext} the quality is measured as perplexity and a lower is better. For other datasets the quality is measured as accuracy and a higher is better. It is important to note, that for LLaMA-family and Qwen-family models the evaluation of \lisa{} and \ows{} approaches was not presented, as memory requirements on the full fine-tuning of these models did not fit to used hardware.

Based on the results provided, the \gpt{} model showed the best performance on commonsense reasoning datasets when using the \lisa{} approach, which led to an 8.1\% improvement in quality over the \lora{} method. However, this approach also resulted in an 18.8\% decrease in quality on the Wikitext task. The \uidl{} method delivered satisfactory outcomes, improving accuracy by 1.1\% and reducing perplexity by 0.4\%. Notably, both \lisa{} and \uidl{} outperformed considered \lora{} modifications, such as \dora{}, \pissa{}, and \adalora{}. In contrast, the \sleb{} approach was associated with the poorest outcomes across all datasets, leading to a 33.7\% degradation in quality on commonsense reasoning tasks and a significant 160.7\% drop in quality on the language modeling task compared to \lora{}. The \shortllama{} method performed comparably to \sleb{}, with a 13.1\% decrease in accuracy and a 131.6\% increase in perplexity. The \ows{} method resulted in a less pronounced decrease in model quality, with a 17.8\% reduction on reasoning datasetes and a 4.1\% reduction on language modeling dataset. These findings suggest that completely removing Transformer blocks from the model (as in the case of \sleb{} and \shortllama{}) results in substantial degradation in quality, particularly in language modeling tasks. This contrasts with methods that only freeze non-essential blocks. Remarkably, the superior performance of \lisa{} can largely be attributed to its strategy of full fine-tuning of all model parameters, rather than lack of "importance" in \gpt{} model.

For the \llamaTwoSmall{} and \llamaTwoBig{} models the \pissa{} approach delivered superior performance across all datasets, with the sole exception of Wikitext. When it comes to importance-based methods, \uidl{} achieved top results by incurring less than a 1\% decrease in quality on commonsense reasoning tasks, while matching the performance of \lora{} on language modeling dataset. In stark contrast, both \sleb{} and \shortllama{} exhibited the poorest performance across all datasets. Specifically, these methods resulted in an average quality decline of 30.5\% on common-sense reasoning tasks. More dramatically, they undermined the \llm{}'s capability to produce lengthy and coherent text, leading to a staggering 1491.9\% degradation in quality on Wikitext. This significant disparity in performance is likely due to the pruning methodologies employed by \sleb{} and \shortllama{}. Unlike other approaches that only freeze non-essential Transformer blocks, allowing them to still transmit information and receive necessary updates, \sleb{} and \shortllama{} completely remove these blocks, eliminating crucial stages for data flow.

Finally, for the \qwenSmall{}, \qwenMedium{}, and \qwenLarge{}  models, the same pattern emerges. The \pissa{} method consistently delivers superior results on the majority of datasets. Supposedly, the reason is that \pissa{} does not initialize adapters with Gaussian noise but rather uses the Singular Value Decomposition of the initial model weights. This strategy transfers the most significant weights directly into the \lora{} adapters, thereby enhancing performance. Specifically, for the \qwenMedium{} model, the best quality results are observed with \adalora{}. Among the importance-based methods, \uidl{} stands out by providing the highest performance, achieving lossless results in commonsense reasoning and language modeling tasks. It even surpasses \pissa{} in some tasks on 1\%. Conversely, the use of \sleb{} and \shortllama{} generally results in a notable decrease in quality, averaging a 28.8\% drop across commonsense reasoning datasets and an astonishing 1858.5\% drop when com-pared to \lora{}. Remarkably, the \qwenSmall{} model fine-tuning with \shortllama{} achieves results comparable to those of \uidl{}, with less than a 1\% quality degradation on ARC-e, Hellaswag, MMLU, Openbook QA, PIQA, and Winogrande datasets. Furthermore, it even exceeds the performance of \lora{} by 2.6\% on the Wikitext dataset.

Therefore, as a result of analysis of the final model quality after the fine-tuning with considered approaches, several conclusions can be made. Firstly, the \lisa{} method, when applied to the \gpt{} model, demonstrated an outstanding performan-ce as full fine-tuning of model parameters can yield substantial benefits compared to \lora{}. Secondly, for LLaMA- and Qwen-family models among \\ importance-based methods the \uidl{} approach provided the best results, leading to less than 1\% of quality decrease compared with \lora{}. Thirdly, significantly poor outcomes were observed with the \sleb{} and \shortllama{} methods, which invol-ve the removal of Transformer blocks. These approaches led to a substantial degradation in quality on average on 34.2\% on commonsense reasoning datasets, and particularly on 1195.5\% in language modeling task. Finally, for the \llamaTwoSmall{} and \llamaTwoBig{} models, the \pissa{} method emerged as particularly effective, outperforming other methods in almost all datasets except Wikitext. This superior performance of \pissa{} can be attributed to its use of Singular Value Decomposition for initializing adapters, which effectively harnesses significant model weights to enhance performance.

Acceleration results when fine-tuning with considered methods are presented in Table \ref{tab:acceleration}. The \textit{Inference time} column is defined as time required to iterate through calibration data to identify the important Transformer blocks for importance-based methods. The \textit{Fine-tuning time} column is defined as time required to fine-tune a pruned model with non-essential Transformer blocks being frozen or removed. The \textit{Fine-tuning time acceleration} column is measured as the ratio of time required to fine-tune a model with \lora{} to the time required to fine-tune a model with chosen approach. The \textit{Cumulative acceleration} column is defined as time required to infer through calibration data, prune non-essential units, and fine-tune the model. Notably, following the article, for the \shortllama{} and \sleb{} approaches the inference was conducted on the 128 samples from validation data, for \uidl{} and \ows{} the full validation sample of \eTwoENLG{} and \alpaca{} datasets for \gpt{} and other models respectively was used. 

Among the evaluated approaches, \sleb{} and \shortllama{} stand out for their efficiency in fine-tuning, as they completely eliminate Transformer blocks from the model structure. These methods result in a significant acceleration of the fine-tuning process, reducing the time by an average of 16\% for the \gpt{} model and 38\% for the LLaMA- and Qwen-family models. However, these methods incur a substantial increase in the time required for inference on calibration data, averaging on 2652.5\% longer for the \gpt{} model and 915.1\% longer for the LLaMA- and Qwen-family models compared to \uidl{}. This increase is due to the necessity of multiple forward passes to assess the impact of pruning on loss or perplexity, which introduces computational overhead. Additionally, the size of the model exacerbates this issue, as larger models with more layers require extended inference times to identify the most critical Transformer blocks. Consequently, for smaller models like \gpt{}, the reduction in fine-tuning time offsets the increased inference time, ultima-tely achieving an average acceleration of 13.1\% on \gpt{} compared to \lora{}. Conversely, for larger models with significantly more parameters, the inference can take up to twice as long as fine-tuning, resulting in a slowdown of up to 110.7\% for the \llamaTwoBig{} model. Among the considered methods, \uidl{} delivers the most consistent results, with an average slowdown of 28.4\% due to the extended time required for both inference and fine-tuning with QLoRA. Finally, methods like \lisa{} and \ows{}, which involve full fine-tuning, do not offer any acceleration benefits compared to \lora{}, which updates fewer than 1\% of the model parameters using $A,B$ adapters.

Thus, based on all of the above, the following conclusions can be drawn.
\begin{itemize}
    \item The \lisa{} approach stands out particularly for the \gpt{} model, where it significantly enhances performance in commonsense reasoning tasks by emplo-ying a full fine-tuning of all model parameters. 
    \item The \uidl{} method shows a robust performance across various models and tasks by even achieving improvements in quality with fewer Transformer blocks than \lora{}. However, it does not provide an acceleration due to additional inference on calibration data and QLoRA fine-tuning. 
    \item \sleb{} and \shortllama{} approaches lead to severe quality degradation across all considered models and datasets, especially on language modeling task. Despite their efficiency in reducing fine-tuning time on average on 38\% due to full Transformer blocks removal, the trade-off in terms of performance is substantial. Moreover, these methods require extensive inference time on calibration data to estimate the importance, which can offset the time saved during the fine-tuning. 
    \item The \ows{} approach have shown potential for acceleration without significanly sacrificing the model quality. However, it still incurs substantial time costs because updates all parameters of chosen Transformer blocks.
\end{itemize}

In conclusion, existing methods for estimating the importance of Transformer blocks have potemtial for enhancing the fine-tuning process. However, there are exist multiple limitations that needs to be solved. To begin with, importance-based pruning of Transformer blocks often results in a significant drop in quality, a problem that persists despite further fine-tuning. Additionally, all the importance-based techniques under consideration, such as \uidl{}, \ows{}, \sleb{}, and \shortllama{}, not only add to the computational burden by necessitating inference on calibration data but also potentially double the time required for fine-tuning. Moreover, the inconsistency in results provided by existing importance heuristics leads to doubts about their validity and their ability to truly reflect the Transformer blocks' importance.
