
\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\protect\numberline{}Introduction}

    The \textbf{relevance} of this work is determined by the pivotal role that Large Language Models (\llm{}s) currently play in various domains including machine learning \cite{machine_learning}, computer vision \cite{computer_vision}, and natural language processing \cite{brown2020languagemodelsfewshotlearners}. These pre-trained LLMs are actively employed in numerous industrial applications, prized for their proficiency in tasks such as in-context learning \cite{brown2020languagemodelsfewshotlearners}, instruction following \cite{instruction_following}, and structured reasoning \cite{reasoning}. However, the capabilities of a pre-trained LLM may not be sufficient to tackle
    more specialized tasks, for example, in medical or scientific domains. This necessitates extensive fine-tuning to tailor the models for specific downstream tasks. However, the fine-tuning process is extremely resource-consuming and demands signi-ficant
    memory and time resources, resulting in low training throughput \cite{malladi2024finetuninglanguagemodelsjust}. This way, the evolvement of novel fine-tuning strategies that reduce time and memory costs without sacrificing model quality is of great importance.

    At the same time, given the vast parameter space of modern LLMs, which results in a significant over-parameterization \cite{overparametrization}, identifying and leveraging the most essen-tial model structures becomes crucial. These structures are vital as they dispropor-tionately impact model performance \cite{yu2024superweightlargelanguage}. Effective utilization of LLMs in various scenarios -- whether pre-training, fine-tuning, or inference -- demands a nuanced understanding of "importance" to discern these critical structures. This requirement extends across multiple optimization strategies, including Neural Architecture Search \cite{LPZero} (NAS), \cite{synflow}, \cite{snip}, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (\peft{}) \cite{adalora}, \cite{lisa}, quantiza-tion \cite{quantization_1}, \cite{int8}, \cite{SqueezeLLM}, and model pruning \cite{uidl}, \cite{sleb}, \cite{wanda}. Each of these strategies aims to optimize different aspects of model architecture, yet they often rely on disparate heuristics for estimating importance, many of which lack direct correlation with model quality \cite{LPZero}.

    Therefore, there are numerous avenues for optimizing the use of \llms{}, particularly through applying methodologies for assessing importance of structures within \llms{}. However, to date, there have been no comprehensive studies that systematically investi-gate methods for importance assessment across various structural levels. Addressing this gap is not only crucial for enhancing model efficiency but also for advancing the methodologies used in model optimization, in particular, in the field of fine-tuning acceleration. 

    The \textbf{practical significance} of this research is that it introduces an innovative fine-tuning strategy for \llms{}, which 
    provides up to 44.8\% of acceleration compared to state-of-the-art \lora{} approach, thus enabling the further deployment of \llms{} in resource-constrained environments.

    The \textbf{novelty} of this work can be summarized as follows:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item This work introduces a comprehensive overview of the most prominent existing methodologies for importance estimation of different structures within the \llms{}. 
        \item This study introduces a new fine-tuning approach for \llms{}, that dynamically assigns \lora{} adapters during the fine-tuning based on importance conver-gence analysis.
    \end{itemize}

    The \textbf{goal} of the present study is to develop a method for accelerating the fine-tuning of \llm{} while maintaining quality, with performance degradation compared to the baseline \lora{} approach being less than 1\%.

    To achieve the goal, the following \textbf{objectives} were formulated: 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Conduct a comprehensive background study on the topic of fine-tuning accele-ration techniques. 
        \item Provide an overview of existing methodologies for estimating the importance of various structures within \llm{}. 
        \item Propose a novel \lora{}-based fine-tuning strategy based on importance analysis.
        \item Offer a detailed evaluation of the proposed method and its hyperparameters among various \llms{}.
        \item Conduct a comparative analysis between the proposed solution and the closest competitors in the field.  
        \item Analyze obtained results and draw conclusion.
    \end{enumerate}

    The study employs a combination of \textbf{methodological approaches} to address the research objectives. Firstly, it adopts a method of theoretical analysis and synthesis of existing literature to establish a solid theoretical foundation for the research. Secondly, programming methods are utilized to analyze the importance of \llms{}'. Lastly, the study employs Deep Learning techniques to fine-tune \llms{}.

    The \textbf{object} of this study is the fine-tuning acceleration of \llms{} through importan-ce analysis of Transformer blocks.
    
    This study was conducted as part of a \textbf{research project at the Huawei} dedicated to the fine-tuning acceleration of \llms{}. The results of this study were presented at the European Conference on Artificial Intelligence 2025 (ECAI, Rank A) and published in the article "On the Influence of Layer Importance on LLM Fine-Tuning Acceleration and Quality" in journal indexed in Scopus (Q4). An individual contribu-tion include the development of the key idea of method, which suggests to perform an importance analysis during the \lora{}-based fine-tuning and attach \lora{} adapters only to the critical Transformer blocks. In addition, an overview of existing competi-tors in the field was performed as well as experiments on applying proposed approach on a wide range of \llms{} with following performance analysis.
