
\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\protect\numberline{}Introduction}

    The \textbf{relevance} of this research lies in the central function that Large Language Models (\llms{}) now serve across a range of fields, such as machine learning \cite{machine_learning}, computer vision \cite{computer_vision}, and natural language processing \cite{brown2020languagemodelsfewshotlearners}. These advanced models, trained on vast datasets, have become foundational components in various commercial and industrial settings, valued for their strong performance in activities like in-context learning \cite{brown2020languagemodelsfewshotlearners}, following complex instructions \cite{instruction_following}, and engaging in structured reasoning cite{reasoning}. Nevertheless, the capabilities of generic, pre-trained LLMs frequently fall short when it comes to highly specialized applications, such as those found in medical or scientific contexts. To bridge this gap, models often undergo a process called fine-tuning, which adapts them to specific downstream tasks. Unfortunately, this adaptation is highly demanding in terms of both computational resources and time. As LLMs have increased in size and complexity, the costs associated with training -- both financial and environmental -- have surged accordingly. Additionally, the energy consumed by the latest generation of LLMs has reached unprecedented levels, raising further concerns about sustainability. For instance, fine-tuning of the DeepSeek-V3 model requires about 3M H800 GPU hours \cite{deepseekai2025deepseekv3technicalreport} and approximately 597 tons of carbon emissions  \cite{aiindex2026}.

    Given these challenges, there is a pressing need for innovative fine-tuning techniques that can reduce resource consumption without compromising model efficacy. Developing such methods is critical for ensuring that advances in LLM technology remain accessible and cost-effective.
    
    At the same time, the enormous parameter counts in today’s LLMs result in significant redundancy, with many parameters contributing little to overall performance \cite{overparametrization}. Thus, identifying and making use of the most influential components of these models is essential \cite{yu2024superweightlargelanguage}. The effectiveness of LLMs -- whether during pre-training, fine-tuning, or inference -- often hinges on recognizing which elements of the model architecture are most impactful. This insight is foundational not only for model optimization strategies like Neural Architecture Search \cite{LPZero}, \cite{synflow}, \cite{snip}, quantization \cite{quantization_1}, \cite{int8}, \cite{SqueezeLLM}, and pruning \cite{uidl}, \cite{sleb}, \cite{wanda}, but also for accelerating and streamlining the fine-tuning process. However, existing approaches to measure the importance of model structures frequently rely on disparate heuristics, many of which do not have a clear or consistent relationship with actual model performance \cite{LPZero}.

    Therefore, there are numerous avenues for optimizing the use of \llms{}, particularly through applying methodologies for assessing importance of structures within \llms{}. However, to date, there have been no comprehensive studies that systematically investigate methods for importance assessment across various structural levels. Addressing this gap is not only crucial for enhancing model efficiency but also for advancing the methodologies used in model optimization, in particular, in the field of fine-tuning acceleration. 

    The \textbf{practical significance} of this research is that it introduces an innovative fine-tuning strategy for \llms{}, which 
    provides up to 44.8\% of acceleration compared to state-of-the-art \lora{} approach, thus enabling the further deployment of \llms{} in resource-constrained environments.

    The \textbf{novelty} of this work can be summarized as follows:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item This work introduces a comprehensive overview of the most prominent existing methodologies for importance estimation of different structures within \llms{}. 
        \item This study introduces a new fine-tuning approach for \llms{}, that dynamically assigns \lora{} adapters during the fine-tuning based on importance analysis.
    \end{itemize}

    The \textbf{goal} of the present study is to develop a method for accelerating the fine-tuning of \llm{} while maintaining quality, with performance degradation compared to the baseline \lora{} approach being less than 1\%.

    To achieve the goal, the following \textbf{objectives} were formulated: 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Carry out an in-depth review of the fine-tuning acceleration approaches, as well as existing strategies used to evaluate the importance of different components within \llm{}. 
        \item Propose a novel \lora{}-based fine-tuning strategy based on importance analysis.
        \item Offer a detailed evaluation of the proposed method and its hyperparameters among various \llms{}.
        \item Conduct a comparative analysis between the proposed solution and the closest competitors in the field.  
        \item Analyze obtained results and draw conclusion.
    \end{enumerate}

    The study employs a combination of \textbf{methodological approaches} to address the research objectives. Initially, it conducts a theoretical review and synthesis of relevant academic works, providing a strong conceptual basis for the investigation. Next, programming techniques are applied to evaluate the importance of \llms{} architectures. Finally, Deep Learning methodologies are utilized to fine-tune \llms{}.

    The \textbf{object} of this study is the fine-tuning acceleration of \llms{} through importan-ce analysis of Transformer blocks.
    
    This study was conducted as a part of a \textbf{research project at the Huawei} aimed at accelerating the fine-tuning of \llms{} with Parameter-Efficient approaches. The results of this research were presented at the European Conference on Artificial Intelligence 2025 (ECAI, Rank A), Neuroinformatics 2024, and published in the articles in journals indexed in Scopus (Q4, Q3). \textbf{An individual contribution} include the development of the key idea of method, which suggests to perform an importance analysis during the \lora{}-based fine-tuning and attach \lora{} adapters only to the critical Transformer blocks. In addition, an overview of existing competitors in the field was performed as well as experiments on applying proposed approach on a wide range of \llms{} with following analysis of obtained results.
