    The discussed idea of estimating the importance of Transformer blocks formed the basis of the proposed method \ourmethodshort{} (\ourmethodsFull{}). \ourmethodshort{} selectively assigns and re-assigns \lora{} adapters based on Transformer blocks impor-tance during the fine-tuning process with subsequent reduction of the number of Transformer blocks.

    \textbf{Hyperparameters:} let \(h\) denote history defined as the number of steps to collect importance statistics, \(\alpha\) denote the ratio of Transformer blocks to be selected for fine-tuning.

    Let us suppose that we are faced with the task of fine-tuning a pre-trained \llm{} (\(\mathcal{M}_{\Phi}\)) on a specific dataset (\(D\)). Initially, the fine-tuning process is divided into several periods, during which the algorithm switches the trainable Transformer
    blocks. Each \ourmethodshort{} period has 3 phases: statistics collection, selection of trainable Transformer blocks, and fine-tuning of the selected blocks. At the beginning of each stage, layer-wise importance statistic is collected during \(h\) steps. Next, an algorithm determines unimportant Transformer blocks and removes them from the selection. After that, importance sampling of Transformer blocks is applied, and the ratio of the most important Transformer blocks (\(\alpha\)) is selected for further fine-tuning. At the same time, \lora{} \(A, B\) adapters for not-trainable Transformer blocks are merged up to the end of the fine-tuning.
    
    As an alternative or a complementary strategy to importance analysis, a conver-gence analysis could be applied to further reduce the number of trainable Transformer blocks. The convergence analysis was not proposed by the author, therefore the detailed explanation of this part of the method can be find in the article \cite{Demidovskij2025}.

    The full \ourmethodshort{} method is described in Algorithm 1.

    \begin{algorithm}
        \caption{\ourmethodshort{} (\(\alpha, h\))}
        \scriptsize
        \begin{algorithmic}[1]
            \State \(D\): fine-tuning dataset
            \State \(\mathcal{M}_{\Phi}\): pre-trained model
            \State \(\alpha\): trainable blocks selection ratio
            \State \(h\): number of accumulation steps
            \State 
            \State \(Q \gets \{\mathcal{M}_{\Phi}\} \): trainable Transformer blocks
            \State \(I \gets \{\}\): accumulated importance scores
            \State \(\mathcal{W} \gets \{\} \): accumulated layer weights
            \For{epoch \(i=1, \dots N\)}
                \For{train step \(i=1, \dots, K\), global batch \(b \in D\)}
                \State \(inp, labels \gets b\): data for forward
                \If{\(i < h\)}
                    \State \textbf{\{Statistics Collection + Fine-tuning Phase\}}
                    \For{layer \(\xi\) in \(\mathcal{M}_{\Phi}\)}
                        \State \(inp, out \gets forward(\xi, inp)\)
                        \State \(I \gets I \cup i(\xi, inp, out)\): calculate layer importance
                        \State \(grads \gets backward(loss(out, labels))\)
                        \State \(apply\_gradients(grads)\)
                    \EndFor
                \ElsIf{\(i=h\)}
                    \State \textbf{\{Selection Phase\}}
                    \For{\(\mathcal{T}\) in \(Q\)}
                        \State \(I_\mathcal{T} \gets I(\{w_j: w_j \in \mathcal{T}\})\): aggregate importance of Transformer blocks (\ref{eq:aggregation})
                    \EndFor
                    \State \(P \gets softmax(I_{\mathcal{T}})\): select with (\ref{eq:probability})
                    \State \(T \gets \alpha * top\_p(Q, P)\): (\ref{eq:top-p-sampling})
                    \State \(Unfreeze(R)\)
                    \State \(Freeze(Q \setminus R)\)
                    \State \(Q \gets R\)
                \Else
                    \State \textbf{\{Fine-tuning Phase\}}
                    \State \(inp, out \gets forward(inp)\)
                    \State \(grads \gets backward(loss(out, labels))\)
                    \State \(apply\_gradients(grads)\)
                \EndIf
                \EndFor
            \EndFor
        \end{algorithmic}
    \end{algorithm}
    \subsection{Importance-based Transformer blocks selection}
        \subsubsection{Statistics collection phase} The first phase of \ourmethodshort{} is dedicated to statistics collection over several iterations of fine-tuning.
            \paragraph{Step 1: Importance metric selection} According to the results of the first year's Course work, cosine similarity importance metric, which is employed in \uidl{}, is the preffered option as this approach demonstrated robust performance after Transformer blocks pruning across different \llm{} scales and a broad range of tasks. The results of estimation of various importance metrics are presented in Table . 
            
            Moreover, the cosine similarity focuses on the direction of vectors rather than their magnitude, which allows to preserve Transformer blocks that contain essential semantic information for LLM performance. Therefore, the cosine similarity was applied as an importance estimation method in the proposed approach. 
            \paragraph{Step 2: Statistics collection} During \(h\) steps of fine-tuning, importance scores are accumulated for each layer of the model. The cosine similarity improtance score is calculated between input and output of each Transformer blocks within an \llm{}. 
        \subsubsection{Selection phase} 
            \paragraph{Step 3: Importance scores aggregation} Upon reaching the \(h\) number of steps, the importance scores of each Transformer block are averaged.
            \paragraph{Step 4: Probability calculation} Based on the importance scores, selection probability for each Transformer block is computed (\ref{eq:probability}). 
            \begin{equation}\label{eq:probability}
                P(T) = \frac{e^{I(T)}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{M}_\Phi}e^{I(k)}},
            \end{equation}
            where \(I\) denotes Transformer block importance score.
            \paragraph{Step 5: Top-P sampling} Next, the Top-P sampling (\ref{eq:top-p-sampling}) is applied, which samples Transformer blocks with the highest probability scores until the sum of the scores reaches the specified threshold value. 
            \begin{equation}\label{eq:top-p-sampling}
                Top_P = \{T_k \in \mathcal{M}_\Phi : \sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{M}_\Phi}P(T_k)\le p\},
            \end{equation}
            where \(p\) denotes the threshold.
            \paragraph{Step 6: Transformer blocks selection} Then, the sampling probability for each Transformer block is recalculated (\ref{eq:probability}). Finally, \(\alpha\) of the most important Transformer blocks are selected for further fine-tuning. 
    Concurrently, for non-trainable Transformer blocks \lora{} \(A, B\) adapters are merged up to the end of the fine-tuning. Merging non-updatable adapters allows to further accelerate the fine-tuning process and increase memory reduction. Obviously, with each training period, the number of trainable Transformer blocks diminishes. As a result, fine-tuning stops once there are no more trainable Transformer blocks. 

    After the fine-tuning phase \lora{} \(A, B\) adapters are merged inside the original \llm{} for the following deployment. 

    \subsection{Key differences from existing approaches}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Existing approaches utilize importance-based pruning of Transformer blocks on inference, introducing considerable quality degradation. In contrast, \ourmethodshort{} integrates the pruning process into the Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning with \lora{}, thereby preventing performance degradation.
            \item Prior methods incur extra computational costs by requiring an additional infe-rence on calibration data for importance estimation. Conversely, \ourmethodshort{} identifies essential Transformer blocks during the fine-tuning process without additional overhead and calibration data. 
            \item Current state-of-the-art approaches manually attach the \lora{} adapters to all Transformer blocks before the fine-tuning process. On the contrary, \ourmethodshort{} offers an innovative approach that enables automatic assignment of \lora{} \(A, B\) adapters only to important Transformer blocks. 
        \end{itemize}
