\subsection{Results}
    This work is the result of a two-year research study, which was aimed to address to primary objectives:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Conduct a comprehensive analysis of current methods used to assess importance of different sturctures within an \llm{}, focusing on the evaluation of critical Transformer blocks to enhance the efficiency of the fine-tuning.
        \item Introduce a novel fine-tuning approach for \llms{}, that dynamically assigns \lora{} adapters during the fine-tuning based on importance analysis of Transfor-mer blocks.
    \end{itemize}

    Throughout the research, various modern approaches for reducing time and memo-ry overhead during the fine-tuning were considered with LoRA de facto being the state-of-the-art solution in the field. However, \lora{} can be substantially optimized by estimating the importance of Transformer blocks within the \llm{}. The concept of \textit{importance} was deemed as the identification of structures that can be removed without significantly affecting model performance. It was found, that existing impor-tance-based compression techniques are mostly utilized during inference, leading to considerable quality degradation. In addition, they provide substantial costs due to additional inference on calibration data.


    To address the aforementioned challenges, the following results were obtained:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The result of the first year's study is a comprehensive overview of existing approaches to assess the importance of Transformer blocks, followed by the conclusion that cosine similarity is the preffered option in terms of quality \cite{Novikova2026}.
        \item The result of the second year's study is the development of a novel \lora{}-based fine-tuning approach, \ourmethodshort{}, that utilizes importance estimation for efficient reallocation of \lora{} adapters.
    \end{itemize}

    During the evaluation, as a baseline, state-of-the-art \lora{} approach has been used. In addition, quality and acceleration results of such modifications of \lora{} as \dora{}, \pissa{}, and \adalora{}, were added for reference. 

    The fine-tuning quality of \ourmethodshort{} was estimated on 6 \llms{} of different scale, including \gpt{}, \llamaTwoSmall{}, \llamaTwoBig{}, \qwenSmall{}, \qwenMedium{}, and \qwenLarge{}. After the fine-tuning, zero-shot accuracy was measured on commonsense reasoning datasets and perplexity was measured on WikiText2 (details are provided in Section \ref{subsec:protocol}).

    The results showed that:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Proposed \ourmethodshort{} demonstrates a notable acceleration of 29\% for \qwenSmall{}, 33\% for \qwenMedium{}, 29\% for \llamaTwoSmall{}, 45\% for \llamaTwoBig{}, and 38\% for \qwenLarge{} without sacrificing the accuracy compared to baseline \lora{} approach. 
        \item Proposed \ourmethodshort{} provides a considerable speedup compared to all considered reference fine-tuning acceleration methods. 
    \end{itemize}

\subsection{Relevance}
    This research was carried out within a project at Huawei, with the objective of accelerating the fine-tuning of \llms{}. The results of this work were presented at the European Conference on Artificial Intelligence 2025 (ECAI, Rank A), Neuroinformatics 2024, and published in the articles in journals indexed in Scopus (Q4, Q3). An individual contribution involves formulating the main concept of the proposed \ourmethodshort{} method, which proposes to attach \lora{} \(A, B\) adapters based on the importance analysis during the fine-tuning process. The second innovation of the method -- convergence analysis -- was not proposed by an author. 

\subsection{Future directions}
    Work in this area is ongoing, with efforts focused on further developing the proposed solution \ourmethodshort{} for pre-training and Reinforcement Learning tasks.
