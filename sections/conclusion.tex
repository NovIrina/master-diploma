\subsection{Results}
    This work is the result of a two-year research study, which was aimed to address to primary objectives:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Provide an in-depth examination of existing importance estimation techniques across various scenarios, with a particular focus on the importance estimation of Transformer blocks to optimize the fine-tuning process.
        \item Introduce a novel fine-tuning approach for \llms{}, that dynamically assigns \lora{} adapters during the fine-tuning based on importance analysis of Transfor-mer blocks.
    \end{itemize}

    Throughout the research, various modern approaches for reducing time and memo-ry overhead during the fine-tuning were considered with LoRA de facto being the state-of-the-art solution in the field. However, \lora{} can be substantially optimized by estimating the importance of Transformer blocks within the \llm{}. The concept of "importance" was deemed as the identification of structures that can be removed without significantly affecting model performance. It was found, that existing impor-tance-based compression techniques are mostly utilized during inference, leading to considerable quality degradation. In addition, they provide substantial costs due to additional inference on calibration data.


    To address the aforementioned challenges, the following results were obtained:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The result of the first year's study is a comprehensive overview of existing approaches to assess the importance of Transformer blocks, followed by the conclusion that cosine similarity is the preffered option in terms of quality.
        \item The result of the second year's study is the development of a novel \lora{}-based fine-tuning approach, \ourmethodshort{}, that utilizes importance estimation for efficient reallocation of \lora{} adapters.
    \end{itemize}

    During the evaluation, as a baseline, state-of-the-art \lora{} approach has been used. In addition, quality and acceleration results of the most prominent modifications of \lora{} -- \dora{}, \pissa{}, and \adalora{} -- were added for reference. 

    The 6 models' fine-tuning was estimated with considered approaches, including \gpt{}, \llamaTwoSmall{}, \llamaTwoBig{}, \qwenSmall{}, \qwenMedium{}, and \qwenLarge{}. After the fine-tuning, in order to estimate the final quality of the model, zero-shot accuracy was measured on commonsense reasoning datasets (details are provided in Section \ref{subsec:protocol}).

    The results showed that:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Proposed \ourmethodshort{} demonstrates a notable acceleration of 29\% for \qwenSmall{}, 33\% for \qwenMedium{}, 29\% for \llamaTwoSmall{}, 45\% for \llamaTwoBig{}, and 38\% for \qwenLarge{} without sacrificing the accuracy compared to baseline \lora{} approach. 
        \item Proposed \ourmethodshort{} significantly outperforms all considered reference impor-tance estimation and fine-tuning acceleration approaches from the point of fine-tuning time and quality. 
    \end{itemize}

\subsection{Relevance}
    This study was conducted as a part of a research project at the Huawei aimed at accelerating the fine-tuning of \llms{}. The results of this study were presented at the European Conference on Artificial Intelligence 2025 (ECAI, Rank A), Neuroinformatics 2024, and published in the articles in journals indexed in Scopus (Q4, Q3). An individual contribution involves formulating the main concept of the proposed \ourmethodshort{} method, which proposes to attach \lora{} \(A, B\) adapters based on the importance analysis during the fine-tuning process. The second innovation of the method -- convergence analysis -- was not proposed by an author. 

\subsection{Future directions}
    Work in this area is ongoing, with efforts focused on further developing the proposed solution \ourmethodshort{} for pre-training and Reinforcement Learning tasks.
